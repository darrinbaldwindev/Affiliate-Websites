## Important evidence notes

### Dscout
Dscout currently presents itself as a paid research platform for Scouts. Its participant page describes missions including app testing, retail environments, prototypes, interviews and website assessments, with PayPal payment after completion. Current Dscout material reports 20K+ missions completed in 2025 and $13M cash distributed in 2025; these are company-reported platform claims, not independently audited figures. A current study page demonstrates a $225–$275 incentive for a two-hour professional study, illustrating upside without treating it as a standard rate. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Dscout also currently operates Dscout XT, an opt-in browser extension that matches users with paid research opportunities while they browse/search/shop. Because this involves browsing/activity data, the USA site should give it a higher privacy-friction flag than ordinary surveys. Dscout states that users can opt out and describes CCPA compliance and security certifications. citeturn0search2

A current Dscout referral program exists for US Scouts aged 13–24: $5 for each referred person who completes and gets paid for a mission, up to $50. This is a participant referral program, not evidence of a publisher affiliate program. citeturn1search13

### PlaybookUX
PlaybookUX currently offers participants unmoderated and moderated UX studies and says participants are paid via PayPal. Its tester FAQ currently states $10 for a 10–15 minute unmoderated session, $30 for a 30-minute moderated interview, $60 for a 60-minute moderated interview, plus $2 card-sort and tree-test payments. These are published tester rates, not publisher affiliate economics. citeturn0search15turn1search6

The current platform says participant access is worldwide and its research product has more than 7 million built-in participants. That latter figure is a company platform claim and should be labelled accordingly if used editorially. citeturn1search4turn1search11

### uTest
uTest currently pays testers for approved bugs, test cases, usability studies, on-site testing and other tasks. A current US project lists $150–$200 for an estimated 3–4 hour task and a $10 bonus for a successful referral, while stressing that eligibility is project-specific. This demonstrates that uTest can provide materially different opportunities from ordinary surveys, but it must not be presented as a guaranteed rate. citeturn0search6turn0search13

The $10 amount is tied to the specific project referral and is not evidence of a general publisher affiliate payout. citeturn0search13

### TestingTime
TestingTime currently recruits paid test users for apps, websites, physical products and services. Its current participant page says studies may be remote or in person and a separate current page gives an average study compensation of €40/£50/CHF50 on that international service page. Its current FAQ also explicitly warns that invitations are irregular and participation is not a full source of income. Treat the published compensation as illustrative and geography/currency dependent. citeturn1search0turn1search2turn1search19

### Userlytics
Current Userlytics support confirms participant tests are paid after approval, with automatic PayPal payments every two weeks and approximately a 15-day timing window after completion/approval. This supports inclusion as a legitimate UX-testing candidate, but current publisher affiliate economics remain UNKNOWN. citeturn0search20

### CloudResearch Connect
Current CloudResearch registration material confirms Connect is a free participant account for taking studies and earning money through surveys and interviews. Publisher affiliate economics were not established in this research pass. citeturn1search15

## Ranking impact

1. **Dscout — promote to A candidate**: unusually strong consumer utility, higher-value research formats, and a differentiated privacy/matching story. Affiliate relationship still needs verification.
2. **PlaybookUX — promote to A candidate**: transparent participant compensation and strong UX-testing content depth; publisher economics still unknown.
3. **uTest — promote to A/B**: strong testing depth and live US evidence, but project availability and referral economics are variable.
4. **TestingTime — B**: strong product-testing proposition, but geographic/currency applicability to US traffic needs explicit verification.
5. **Userlytics — B**: legitimate testing candidate; insufficient current affiliate evidence.
6. **CloudResearch Connect — B**: strong consumer research utility; monetisation pathway not yet verified.

## Publication controls

Do not publish a guaranteed earnings figure, average earnings claim, approval claim or affiliate payout unless current evidence supports that exact claim. Participant referral rewards must remain separate from publisher affiliate economics. Individual study examples must be labelled as examples, not standard rates.
