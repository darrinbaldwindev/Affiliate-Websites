# Australia Affiliate Program Priority Research

**Research date:** 2026-09-03
**Purpose:** Prioritise the first affiliate/reward programs for the Australian implementation of Affiliate Websites.

## Executive recommendation

The AU site should not launch with a large undifferentiated directory. The strongest first portfolio is a small, trusted set spanning:

1. **Cashback / shopping rewards** — ShopBack
2. **Paid research / surveys** — Octopus Group, Ipsos iSay, Prolific
3. **User testing** — UserTesting
4. **Earn-more / task platforms** — ySense, Freecash (subject to current AU eligibility/terms)
5. **Retail / buying guides** — THE ICONIC, ASICS, Under Armour, INTERSPORT, My Muscle Chef, Linen House, OzMobiles and selected Commission Factory merchants
6. **Travel** — Booking.com / Trip.com and selected travel programs, once the travel category is built

The commercial model should distinguish **consumer reward/referral programs** from **publisher affiliate programs**. A user-facing recommendation can be useful even when the program does not offer a conventional publisher affiliate relationship, but it must not be represented as affiliate income to the site unless an actual publisher agreement exists.

## Ranking methodology

Priority is based on:

- AU eligibility and local relevance
- consumer trust / brand strength
- clear value proposition
- repeat-use potential
- likely conversion intent
- commission/reward economics where verified
- availability of product feeds / deep links / useful creative
- tracking and attribution practicality
- breadth of content opportunities
- fit with the site's "earn, save, compare" architecture
- compliance / policy risk

Headline commission alone is **not** a ranking criterion. A high percentage on a low-converting or restricted program is less valuable than a lower rate on a trusted, high-intent program.

## Networks to establish

### Commission Factory — highest priority

Commission Factory is the most important initial network for the AU retail/content layer. Its current platform advertises 600+ advertisers across fashion, electronics, travel, health, finance and other major verticals, with AU brands including ASICS, lululemon, My Muscle Chef, Priceline Pharmacy, Under Armour and Samsung. It should be treated as a **merchant acquisition channel**, not a consumer-facing program itself.

### Other networks

After Commission Factory, evaluate Impact, Awin, Rakuten Advertising and Partnerize for merchants that are not available through the AU-first network. The network choice should be determined by actual merchant availability and commercial terms rather than network brand recognition.

## Important distinction: consumer referral vs publisher affiliate

The site will contain two related but separate commercial objects:

### A. Consumer reward/referral program

Example: Octopus Group or Ipsos iSay referral. The user is joining a service to earn rewards. If the site has an approved referral arrangement, the site may earn a referral benefit subject to the program's rules.

### B. Publisher affiliate program

Example: ASICS or Under Armour through Commission Factory. The site sends a qualified shopper to the merchant and receives a commission when an eligible transaction is attributed.

The database and UI must record the distinction explicitly. Never infer an affiliate relationship from the existence of a public referral URL.

## Evidence rules

Every program record should contain:

- program name
- category
- country eligibility
- program type: `CONSUMER_REFERRAL` or `PUBLISHER_AFFILIATE`
- network / direct relationship
- current commission/reward, if verified
- attribution window
- validation period, if available
- payout/reward method
- eligibility restrictions
- promotional restrictions
- last verified date
- source URL(s)
- evidence state: `VERIFIED`, `CLAIMED`, or `UNKNOWN`

Volatile figures such as cashback rates, referral bonuses and commission rates should **never** be hard-coded into evergreen editorial copy without a freshness mechanism.

## Strategic conclusion

The AU site should launch with **quality and trust rather than quantity**. A 15-program verified portfolio is more useful than a 200-program directory full of stale rates and weak offers. The first commercial UX should be built around the user's goal — **Earn, Save, or Compare** — and then present the best verified option for that goal.

The next research stage should turn this shortlist into a structured merchant/program dataset with current application URLs, exact terms, restrictions, product/feed availability, estimated commercial value, and verification timestamps. Only verified records should become live commercial CTAs.
